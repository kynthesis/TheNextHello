# Branching story and choice ledger

**Status:** Proposed implementation of the creator's requirement that choices change story events. Companion to [the GDD](GDD.md). Tachibana Asahi, 29, and Yukimura Mizuki, 23, are confirmed; supporting cast and scene details remain proposals.

## Structure

The opening is shared. A mutually agreed activity in act two opens one of three central paths: Game-first, Private connection, or Shared lives. This is a diegetic invitation, not a route-selection screen. Each path has distinct scenes in acts three and four. At act three's relationship conversation the pair may agree to change how they spend time, allowing one authored path transition. Earlier events remain true; the transition cannot recover missed events.

Friendship and separation can be chosen on any path. Changing relationship mode is separate from changing the activity path. No final decision grants access to events whose prerequisites never occurred.

## Eight major decisions

All options below require clearly presented context. Mizuki can accept, decline, or counter-propose based on established needs. The player chooses Asahi's actions, not Mizuki's feelings or career.

| ID | Decision and immediate event | Persistent fact | At least two later event/action consequences |
|---|---|---|---|
| D01 | Choose the next shared activity: join a guild project, offer a private creative collaboration, or introduce everyday friends and discuss a visit. Play a different follow-up scene for each. | Central path and first mutual commitment. | Distinct act-three event; distinct act-four conflict or opportunity. A guild leadership scene, collaborative deadline, and early visit preparation are separate content, not interchangeable dialogue. |
| D02 | A genuinely overlapping commitment: keep Natsumi's showcase plan, renegotiate before agreeing to Mizuki's event, or knowingly double-book and later choose what to miss. Play the attended event. | Attended, renegotiated, and missed events remain recorded. | Natsumi either offers a later planning/help scene or requires a friendship repair first; Mizuki's alternative event either happens, is replaced with a rescheduled activity, or creates a broken-promise conversation. Declining honestly never counts as breaking a promise. |
| D03 | Share Mizuki's private work-in-progress image after obtaining permission, keep it private, or publish despite an explicit request not to. Confirmation states the audience and the known boundary. | Permission, publication, and third-party exposure. | Consensual publication can open a group creative session; keeping it private preserves a private-only collaboration; violating privacy cancels that session and requires an accountability scene before further sharing. Deleting the post cannot erase who saw it. |
| D04 | A work assignment overlaps the proposed visit: accept the assignment, request a feasible adjustment, or decline after discussing the stated work consequence. Play the work response. | Assignment responsibility and approved leave/availability. | Visit dates or host arrangements change; a later work handoff or career conversation becomes available or closes. Honest unavailability never reduces generic affection. Adjustment can be refused; there is no universal cost-free option. |
| D05 | With a mutually feasible date, agree to an early Hokkaido visit, propose a later visit, or keep the relationship online for now. Play travel preparation, revised planning, or expectations discussion. | Early/late/postponed meeting branch. | Early visit opens act-four meeting and act-five return-to-distance scenes; late visit preserves first-meeting anticipation and opens act-six arrival; online-only opens a practical continuation conversation and closes physical-meeting scenes unless a later explicit plan changes it. |
| D06 | When a triggered issue is raised, take responsibility and negotiate repair, request space with an agreed follow-up, or explicitly refuse to address the issue. Play that conversation rather than a generic argument. | Named issue, repair action, agreed pause, or unresolved rupture. | Repair opens a rebuilding activity appropriate to the original issue; agreed space replaces a planned activity with a follow-up scene; refusal closes the affected intimacy/activity and opens separation. If no preventable rupture occurred, use the equivalent changing-needs negotiation, with separate event outcomes. |
| D07 | Once both discuss feelings, pursue romance if mutual, choose friendship if mutual, or end contact. Play an actual date, friend activity, or farewell. | Explicit relationship mode and continuation agreement. | Subsequent invitations and introductions become romantic, platonic, or unavailable; final planning and meeting scenes differ. Romance cannot be restored merely by choosing a romantic ending label. |
| D08 | Respond to Mizuki's independently made work decision: propose a feasible new arrangement, maintain the current arrangement if mutual, or acknowledge incompatible needs. Play joint planning or separation. | Workable plan for the current mode, or acknowledged incompatibility. | A scheduled follow-through event and the final act's available proposals change; incompatible romance can lead to an explicit friendship proposal or separation. The epilogue follows the plan actually enacted. |

The ledger is a minimum content contract. Major decisions do not all appear in every run: separation closes later romantic decisions and receives a complete resolution. Authoring must supply non-romantic work/friend follow-through where appropriate rather than forcing renewed pursuit of Mizuki.

## Observable divergence examples

**Run A:** Asahi joins the guild project, keeps Natsumi's showcase commitment, and postpones travel around work. The player experiences guild disagreement, Natsumi's event, and a late first meeting. Mizuki has not already met him in act five.

**Run B:** Asahi and Mizuki plan a feasible early visit and agree to introduce friends. The player experiences preparations, meeting in Hokkaido, and the difficult return to separate routines. The final act concerns a next visit or practical future, not the same first greeting as Run A.

**Run C:** Asahi collaborates privately with Mizuki but knowingly publishes an image without permission. The collaboration is canceled. Repair may rebuild their relationship through a different activity, but the canceled event and disclosure remain part of their story. Refusing repair can end the relationship before a visit.

These paths must differ in playable events even if two eventually reach the same ending family.

## Verification contract

For each decision, save an identical pre-choice fixture and follow each available option. Record next scene ID, persistent facts, two later event/action differences, excluded scenes, and final-state validity. A changed line, caption, or music cue alone fails the major-choice test.

Validate all route transitions, early/late/no-meeting states, friend resolutions, and intentional separation. No scene may require an optional clue without a fallback source. No unresolved choice set can have zero available options. Re-entering an app must never replay an event's state effects.

After a completed run, a spoiler-gated history can explain “You kept the showcase commitment; Natsumi later helped with the visit.” Do not reveal alternate-path content until the player requests it. The normal playthrough communicates consequences through people and events, without exposing numeric affection scores.
